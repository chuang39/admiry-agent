# Admiry — marketing plumbing for the agent era

**Tell Admiry what you do. It finds your customers.**

Admiry Agent · Investor one-pager · 2026-09-24 · Confidential draft

## The problem

Marketing is a data-driven, complex system. Large advertisers have agencies, platform reps and measurement teams. The 36.2 million US small businesses get none of that; half have nobody dedicated to marketing. The owner does not know where to buy, has no data, gets poor results, and cannot sustain or scale what works. Today's answer is eight tools and vendors, and nobody owns the result.

## Why now

Meta Muse launched on 2026-09-08 and opened third-party connectors on 2026-09-18; ChatGPT and Claude take the same connector through MCP, an open standard. 45% of US consumers used AI tools for local recommendations in 2026, up from 6% a year earlier. An assistant can talk, plan and analyze; it cannot buy media, host a landing page, update a Google Business Profile, answer a tracked phone line or reconcile spend to bookings. That plumbing does not exist for the long tail.

## What Admiry is

The marketing infrastructure behind the owner's personal agent: the assistant is the interface and the analyst; Admiry is the pipes that execute and measure, exposed as one connector to Meta Muse first, then ChatGPT, Claude and any MCP-capable assistant (Grok and others).

| Pipe | What Admiry runs |
|:-----|:------------------------|
| Channels and creative | Google Search and Meta in the owner's own accounts, later the Admiry Network (our own ad platform); creative from the assistant or Admiry, policy-checked, sized, uploaded |
| Presence and landing | Google Business Profile sync, review replies, discoverability on Google, Maps and AI search; a hosted storefront with a page per service, or the owner's site |
| Conversion | Tracked calls, forms, bookings and orders; leads to the owner in a minute; conversions fed back to Google and Meta |
| Data | One ledger of spend, leads, bookings and revenue, returned to the assistant in plain English for analysis and advice |

**How it works.** "I run a dental practice in San Jose. I want more Invisalign patients." Admiry proposes one plan (budget, pages, tracking, lead estimate); the owner approves once; pages go live, tracking turns on, campaigns launch in the owner's accounts. Weekly: "$318 spent, 11 calls, 4 booked. Google brought 8 of 11. Move $50 a week to Google?" A monthly limit no assistant can raise, an out-of-band code for spend increases, a full audit trail.

## Business model

**Free for the small business; the profit is the platform's.** No subscription, no percentage of Google or Meta spend, no markup: Google and Meta bill the owner directly, and a prepaid top-up carries only its payment cost, at cost. The agent is our acquisition and data layer: budgeted, not expected to pay for itself.

| Where the money is | Mechanism and timing (est.) |
|:-----|:------------------------|
| Mid-size and large advertisers | Our own DSP: the profit engine once the platform is live with SSP supply and has data (outside this document) |
| Admiry Network media | Same platform, priced like any external platform and labelled to the owner; only when its cost per lead beats the best channel; at most 20% of a plan. Month 4–8 after live supply |
| Later services | Add-ons, payments, leads, referrals: priced separately when introduced; today's $0 covers today's services |

Cost to serve ≈ $11 per business per month; burn ≈ $1.1k per month at the 100-business cap. Where the Network wins it offsets that: break-even at ≈ 4.5% of an average $900 plan, ≈ +$38 per business per month at the 20% cap.

## Market

US local advertising: $186 billion in 2026. Nine million small businesses already make ad creative with Meta's AI tools; 59% expect acquiring customers to be a challenge this year. Beachhead: single-location US service businesses spending $300–5,000 a month, whose next customer is a call, a form or a booking. Distribution is the assistant; the last mile is ours.
