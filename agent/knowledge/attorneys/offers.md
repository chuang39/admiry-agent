# Attorneys — what the ads lead with

What law firms lead with in their ads, and the bar rules each offer brings.

## Key points

- Lead with the firm's own offer, as the owner told Admiry: a free consultation, flat fees, languages spoken, evening or weekend hours.
- Flat-fee packages suit estate planning, traffic tickets and business formation [research-verticals].
- Any fee in the ad, contingent ("no fee unless we recover") or flat, says whether the client also pays costs: California and Texas require it for contingency fees, Florida for every fee [ca-bp-6157-2] [tx-bar-ads] [fl-bar-handbook].
- Leave past results and "as seen on" claims out of the ad text; in California an electronic-media ad portraying a result needs a disclosure [ca-bp-6158-3].

## Details

Start from what the owner told Admiry; never make up a fee, a free consultation or any other offer.

What a matter is worth decides how much an offer can cost; the Numbers give the estimates by practice [research-verticals]. The research document lists flat-fee packages for wills and trusts, a flat-fee price list for traffic tickets and flat-fee formation packages for business law [research-verticals].

Fees and costs. California asks an ad that offers representation on a contingent basis to say whether the client is responsible for costs advanced when nothing is recovered; if the client never owes costs, no statement is needed [ca-bp-6157-2]. Texas asks a contingent-fee ad to say whether the client pays other expenses, such as litigation costs, and an advertised fee binds the lawyer while the ad runs, unless it names a shorter period, for up to a year [tx-bar-ads]. Florida asks any ad with fee information, flat or contingent, to disclose the fees and costs the client may owe, and to honor an advertised fee for at least 90 days unless the ad names a shorter period [fl-bar-handbook].

Results. California asks an electronic-media ad that portrays a result in a case to give the circumstances, or to say the result depended on that case's facts and results will differ if the facts differ [ca-bp-6158-3]. Admiry's drafts leave results and "as seen on" claims out of the ad text altogether.

Awards. California bars naming an award or recognition that membership conferred or whose giver charges a fee [ca-bp-6157-2]; Florida wants a bona fide organization with objective criteria, and the award's exact name, its giver and its year [fl-bar-handbook]. A draft names an award only as the owner gave it, with those three.

Round-the-clock intake for arrests, DUI and injuries is offered only when someone answers. A free case-review form belongs on the page [research-verticals].

## Numbers

- Fees per matter (estimates): an estate plan $1,500–$5,000; a DUI case $2,000–$10,000; a family case $5,000–$30,000; a personal-injury fee per case $3,000–$30,000 and up, the contingency share varying [research-verticals].

## Do not say

- "Free consultation" when it is not free.
- "No win, no fee" or a flat fee without saying who pays costs.
- A past result, a settlement amount or "as seen on".
- Awards or ratings without exact name, giver and year, or paid for [ca-bp-6157-2] [fl-bar-handbook].
