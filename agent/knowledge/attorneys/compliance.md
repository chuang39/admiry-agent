# Attorneys — what the ads must not say

Bar rules every law firm's ads keep, the states that add their own, and what Google limits.

## Key points

- Every ad names a responsible lawyer or the firm (Texas, Louisiana: a lawyer), contact, the office's city or county (California: address) [aba-7-2] [tx-bar-ads] [la-7-2] [fl-bar-handbook].
- No promised result, unprovable claim or comparison: "we win", "best", "top-rated" [aba-7-1-comment].
- "Specialist" or "certified" only when a body the state accepts certified the lawyer, naming it and the area [aba-7-2].
- "No fee unless we win" says who pays costs (California, Florida, Texas) [ca-bp-6157-2] [fl-bar-handbook] [tx-bar-ads].
- Bankruptcy ads say the firm is a debt relief agency helping people file for bankruptcy relief [usc-11-528].
- Florida, Texas and Louisiana file an ad beyond name, contact, practice areas, languages and consultation fees: keep to those [fl-bar-handbook] [tx-bar-ads] [la-7-2].

## Details

Not legal advice: the state bar has the final word; beyond Florida, Texas, California and Louisiana, states were not checked [research-verticals].

ABA Model Rule 7.1 bars a false or misleading communication [aba-7-1]; its Comment 3 adds that past results and unsubstantiated claims or comparisons can mislead [aba-7-1-comment]. Rule 7.2 has every communication name a responsible lawyer or firm with contact information, and allows a certified-specialist claim only if a state-approved or ABA-accredited body certified the lawyer and the ad names it [aba-7-2].

Filing. Florida, Texas and Louisiana exempt from filing an ad holding only the content their rules list (name, offices, contact, hours, practice areas, admissions, licenses, languages and fees); the Numbers give the deadlines [fl-bar-handbook] [tx-bar-ads] [la-7-2].

Florida. Every ad names a responsible lawyer or the firm and a bona fide office's city or county; fee information discloses the fees and costs the client may owe; a certification claim names the certifying organization and the area; an award comes from a bona fide organization with objective criteria, named with giver and year. The Bar's handbook treats paid search results as advertising [fl-bar-handbook].

Texas. Every ad names a responsible lawyer and the primary practice location; a contingent-fee ad says whether the client pays other expenses; a certification claim is limited to the state's board or a body it accredits [tx-bar-ads].

Louisiana. Every ad gives a responsible lawyer's full name and an office city or town; a filed ad carries the Bar's filing number [la-7-2].

California. The ad names a responsible lawyer or firm and an address [ca-crpc-7], and shows a bona fide office's city or county, or the State Bar address of record [ca-bp-6157-2]. It may not guarantee success or claim immediate cash or quick settlements; a dramatization is disclosed; an award is named only if not conferred by membership or for a fee [ca-bp-6157-2]. An electronic-media ad portraying a result gives the circumstances, or says results differ when the facts differ [ca-bp-6158-3].

Google. The government documents policy lets only certified governments and authorized providers promote direct acquisition of documents such as visas or an EIN; our reading is that a firm's ad offering to obtain one directly may need that certification [g-gov]. The Google Search file has the audience limits and bail-bond ban.

The bankruptcy statement runs to 97 characters and a Google description holds 90 [usc-11-528] [g-rsa], so a bankruptcy plan waits for counsel's shorter, substantially similar wording.

## Numbers

- California: as updated on 2026-01-01, section 6157.2 has every lawyer ad show the city, town or county of a bona fide office, or the lawyer's State Bar address of record [ca-bp-6157-2].
- Florida: internet ads other than the firm's own website are filed with the Bar at least 20 days before first use, unless they hold no illustrations and only the safe-harbor content of Rule 4-7.16 (such as name, office, phone, hours, practice areas, languages, consultation fee) [fl-bar-handbook] [research-verticals].
- Texas: an ad that Rule 7.05 does not exempt is filed with the Advertising Review Committee no later than 10 days after it is disseminated [tx-bar-ads].
- Louisiana: an ad beyond Rule 7.2(b)'s permissible content is filed with the Bar prior to or concurrently with its first dissemination and carries the filing number the Bar assigns (Rules 7.2(a)(3), 7.7 and 7.8; the rules as amended through 2022-01-01) [la-7-2].
- New York: a Joint Order dated 2026-05-27, effective 2026-06-01, amended Rules 1.0, 7.1, 7.3 and 7.4 [ny-amendments]; the State Bar Association's summary says 7.1 and 7.3 now follow the ABA's 2018 rules and 7.4 is folded into 7.1(c), which is why these files carry no New York line [nysba-amendments].

## Do not say

- "Guaranteed", "we win", "quick settlement" or a promised outcome.
- "Specialist" or "expert" without the certifying body.
- "No fee unless we win" without saying whether the client pays costs.
- A past result or an amount recovered.
