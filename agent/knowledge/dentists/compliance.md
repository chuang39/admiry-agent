# Dentists — what the ads must not say

Claims, offers and targeting a dental practice's ads must avoid, and the notices its page must carry.

## Key points

- Never promise a result, comfort or a perfect smile: no "pain-free" or "guaranteed".
- "Specialist" only for a recognized specialty the dentist holds (cosmetic and implant dentistry are not) [ada-specialties].
- North Carolina and Texas ads name each advertised dentist and say general dentist or the specialty, Texas adds the degree (our reading) [nc-dental-ads] [tx-dental-names]; Florida ads give the dentist's license number [fl-dental-id].
- Prices and discounts in Florida, Texas, North Carolina, California or New York, and Florida's free services, need statements: keep them off the ad text [fl-466-019] [tx-dental-fees] [nc-dental-fees] [ca-bp-651] [ny-29-1].
- No patient's name, story or photo without written permission; Texas and California photos say results may vary [cfr-164-508] [tx-dental-claims] [ca-bp-651].

## Details

Not legal advice: the state board has the final word.

Naming the dentist. North Carolina ads name, prominently, each dentist they advertise as a general dentist or a qualified
specialist [nc-dental-ads]. In Texas a general dentist's ad for a specific service puts "General Dentist" after the name
[tx-dental-general]; every professional communication gives the dental degree and "general dentist" or the specialty, and a
trade-name ad names the owners and a dentist at each location [tx-dental-names]. Florida's rule, amended 2026-09-24,
has every ad identify the responsible dentist: license number, or name with the full name, address and phone on file
[fl-dental-id].

Specialist claims. The National Commission recognizes 12 dental specialties, none cosmetic, implant or general dentistry
[ada-specialties]. Texas allows "specialist" for its recognized areas, only with accredited certification; the owner keeps
general dentists from seeming specialists [tx-dental-ads]. Florida lets a dentist announce a practice emphasis outside the
recognized specialties only with a capital-letter statement that it is not a recognized specialty; our reading: "cosmetic
dentist" or "implant dentist" announces one [fl-466-0282]. Florida also bars "sleep dentistry" in ads without a general
anesthesia permit [fl-dental-id].

Florida. An ad for a free, discounted or reduced-fee service carries a capital-letter statement of the patient's right to refuse,
cancel or recover payment for any other service performed within 72 hours of responding to the ad [fl-456-062]. Fee information
needs a "minimum fee only" disclaimer; laudatory statements and unjustified expectations of results are barred [fl-466-019]. The
board adds the procedure's ADA name or code, the fee's period and an offer's dates [fl-dental-fees]. None of it fits a search ad.

Patient stories. HIPAA requires the patient's signed authorization before protected health information is used for marketing
[cfr-164-508]; our reading: this covers identifiable testimonials and patient photos [research-verticals]. Texas adds
"Actual results may vary." to patient photos [tx-dental-claims]. California patient photos name the procedures performed, and
before-and-after views say the same results may not occur for all patients [ca-bp-651].

Notices. The page tells uninsured and self-pay patients they can ask for a good-faith estimate, prominently and searchable
[cfr-149-610].

Google's Health category names surgical procedures and chronic conditions, so our reading is that implant, extraction,
oral-surgery and gum-disease ads cannot use your data segments, Customer Match or lookalike segments [g-pa-health]
[research-verticals].

Reviews. The FTC bars fake reviews, rewarding reviews of a chosen sentiment and suppressing reviews by threats
[ftc-reviews]; endorsements disclose material connections and the generally expected performance [ftc-endorsements].

## Numbers

- The National Commission on Recognition of Dental Specialties and Certifying Boards recognizes 12 dental specialties [ada-specialties].
- Florida's statement applies to dentists (F.S. chapter 466) among other licensed practitioners: F.S. 456.062 [fl-456-062].

## Do not say

- "Pain-free", "guaranteed" or any promised result.
- "Specialist" for cosmetic, implant or general dentistry; Florida: no "cosmetic dentist" or "implant dentist".
- "Best" or "top-rated": a named, dated source, never in California; Florida: no praise.
- A price or discount (Florida, Texas, North Carolina, California, New York); "free" in Florida.
