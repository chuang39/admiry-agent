# Dentists — what the ads lead with

Offers that move dental patients to book, and the rules that come with them.

## Key points

- Lead with the practice's own offer, as the owner told Admiry; never invent one.
- Family and general practices: a new-patient visit, insurance accepted, online booking [research-verticals].
- Implants, Invisalign and cosmetic work: a free consultation and payment plans [research-verticals].
- A payment amount, "<n> months", a down payment or a rate brings credit-advertising disclosures: say "payment plans available" [regz-ads].
- Florida: no prices, discounts or free services in the ad text; Texas, North Carolina, California and New York: no prices or discounts; Texas "free" only when no third party pays [fl-456-062] [fl-466-019] [fl-dental-fees] [tx-dental-fees] [nc-dental-fees] [ca-bp-651] [ny-29-1].

## Details

What a new patient is worth decides how much an offer can cost; the Numbers give the estimates by service. A family or general
practice leads with a new-patient visit, the insurance plans it takes and online booking. Implant, clear-aligner and cosmetic
cases are large, so the offer is a free consultation and payment plans [research-verticals]. Pediatric practices that take
Medicaid or CHIP say so [research-verticals]. A same-day emergency slot is offered only when the practice's hours hold it.

Payment plans. Regulation Z's rule for closed-end credit (12 CFR 1026.24) covers installment plans: an ad states only terms
actually offered, and one that states a payment amount, the number of payments or repayment period, the down payment or the
finance charge also states the down payment, the repayment terms and the annual percentage rate; any rate, "0%" included, is an
annual percentage rate [regz-ads]. Our reading: those terms in a dental ad advertise credit, so the ad says "payment plans
available" and leaves them out.

Prices and offers. Florida: a free or discounted service in an ad needs the long statement described in the compliance file
[fl-456-062]; any fee needs a "minimum fee only" disclaimer [fl-466-019], the procedure's ADA name or code and the fee's period,
or the fee held for 90 days after the last ad [fl-dental-fees]. Texas: an ad with a price names the service and the related services usually required with it, says the fee
is a minimum that may increase, and gives the dates the price holds; a discount gives the standard fee and whether it is cash
only; "free" is barred when a third party such as Medicaid or Medicare pays, and so is saying a copay or deductible is waived,
or insurance taken as payment in full, when it is owed [tx-dental-fees]. California: a price advertisement is exact, without
"as low as", "and up" or similar words ("from" too, our reading); a fee statement other than a standard consultation fee or a
range for specific services discloses all variables and material factors [ca-bp-651]. North Carolina: a price ad names the
related services usually required at extra cost and states a discount's amount; a range needs its basic factors
[nc-dental-fees]. New York: a price ad states any extra charge for integral related services and the period the prices hold
[ny-29-1].

## Numbers

- A new general-dentistry patient's first year: $500–$2,500 (estimate) [research-verticals].
- An implant case $3,000–$30,000; an orthodontic case $3,000–$8,000; a cosmetic case $2,000–$15,000 (estimates) [research-verticals].

## Do not say

- A payment amount, "<n> months", down payment or rate without the credit terms.
- A price or discount (Florida, Texas, North Carolina, California, New York); "free" in Florida.
- An offer, price or discount the owner did not give.
- Waived copays or deductibles, or insurance taken as payment in full.
