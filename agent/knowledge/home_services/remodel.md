# Home services — kitchen and bath remodels

How a remodeler's ads catch kitchen and bathroom projects, and which searches to keep out.

## Key points

- Kitchen and bath projects are consultation-driven: lead with a design consultation (free only if the owner offers it), a gallery and financing [research-verticals].
- Search themes: "kitchen remodel <town>", "bathroom remodeling contractor", "bathroom renovation near me".
- Keep out: remodeling jobs, cabinets and fixtures for sale, do-it-yourself, big-box stores, "remodel ideas".
- Maryland, New Jersey, Pennsylvania, Massachusetts and Connecticut ask for the home-improvement registration number in ads (Maryland calls it a license number; Connecticut also asks the ad to say the business is registered) [md-mhic] [nj-hic] [pa-hicpa] [ma-142a] [ct-20-427].

## Details

A kitchen or bathroom remodel is a large ticket: the research document reads $26,990 and $12,152 (the page now shows $12,153) average projects as justifying a
$165.67 general-contractor cost per lead, and calls the vertical consultation-driven. The cost-per-lead report has no remodeling
category, so its figure is for general contractors; the Numbers give each figure's source and scope [research-verticals]
[liq-home-2026] [ha-kitchen] [ha-bath].

A remodel starts with a design consultation. The research document gives the vertical the call to action "Free consultation" and lists
a before-and-after gallery, design-consult booking, a showroom visit, financing and 3D design among its page modules
[research-verticals]. A consultation is called free only when the owner offers it free.

Financing. Our reading is that an ad quoting a monthly amount for financing brings Regulation Z's credit-advertising disclosures, so
say "financing available" and leave the amount out; the compliance file gives the rule [regz-ads].

Registration numbers. The compliance file lists the five states' rules. Connecticut asks that an advertisement state the fact that the
contractor is registered as well as give the number [ct-20-427]. New Jersey's Division says it registers, and does not license, home
improvement contractors, calls the word "license" for that status deceptive and misleading, and tells them not to use it in any
advertisement [nj-hic-notice]. Maryland's commission page asks for the contractor's name and license number in each home improvement
advertisement, and adds rules on the price in an ad (a price that leaves out items needed for the product to work and look right, or
a delivery or installation charge, must say so), on discount coupons (no requirement to present one before an estimate) and on gifts
as an inducement to make a contract; the Commission does not count a sale or a price reduction as a gift [md-mhic].

## Numbers

- Average cost per lead in search advertising in the Construction & Contractors (General) category: $165.67, the third-highest of the report's 16 home-services categories, with the lowest conversion rate of the 16, 2.61 %; the report has no kitchen or bathroom remodeling category (LocaliQ's 2025 home-services report: its customers' US search campaigns on Google Ads and Microsoft Ads, 3,211 in all, at least 103 in each category, April 2024 to March 2025; its "averages" are medians) [liq-home-2026].
- A kitchen remodel: $26,990 on average, in a normal range of $14,591–$41,617 (HomeAdvisor's cost guide, page updated June 2026; the page names no region for the average and shows cost ranges for eight U.S. regions) [ha-kitchen].
- A bathroom remodel: most homeowners will spend about $12,153, typically $6,651–$17,660 (HomeAdvisor's cost guide, page updated June 2026, read 2026-10-15; the page does not call $12,153 an average and names no region) [ha-bath].

## Do not say

- "Done in <n> days" unless every project is.
- "Licensed" for a New Jersey home-improvement registration; say "registered" [nj-hic-notice].
