# Home services — landscaping

How a landscaper's ads catch design projects and recurring lawn care, and which searches to keep out.

## Key points

- Design-build projects lead with a consultation and a gallery; lawn care with a quote and a recurring plan [research-verticals].
- Start the ads before the owner's busy season (usually spring, our reading).
- Search themes: "landscaping <town>", "landscape design", "lawn care service near me"; "hardscaping" and "tree removal" only if the owner does them.
- Keep out: landscaping jobs, plants and mulch for sale, mowers, "landscaping ideas".
- Maryland, New Jersey, Pennsylvania and Connecticut count landscape projects as home improvement: the registration number (Maryland: the license number) in ads [md-mhic] [nj-hic] [pa-hicpa] [ct-20-427].

## Details

A design-build project and recurring lawn care are two different jobs. The research document gives landscaping the call to action "Free
consultation" and lists a project gallery, design-consult booking, maintenance plans and a plant warranty among its page modules; it
gives lawn care "Request a quote" and lists an instant quote by lot size, recurring plans, fertilization and weed programs and online
billing [research-verticals]. A consultation is called free only when the owner offers it free.

Lawn care services typically include fertilization, pesticide application and herbicide application, among other work [ha-lawn]. The
research document lists pesticide-applicator licensing for weed and feed treatments among lawn care's required disclosures, and a
landscape contractor license in some states (California for one) among landscaping's; it marks both unverified [research-verticals].
California's licensing board says a landscaping project of $1,000 or more in labor and materials needs a contractor license
[cslb-landscaping], and Florida counts weed control on a lawn as pest control (by its definitions), which needs the state's license [fl-482-021]
[fl-482-071]; elsewhere, ask. Where a state asks for a license number in ads, the compliance file's rule holds.

California's Automatic Renewal Law asks for clear disclosure of the renewal terms and the customer's consent before charging, the
research document says, and applies to maintenance-plan memberships; our reading is that a recurring lawn care plan that renews
itself counts; the terms and consent belong on the sign-up step, not in the ad (our reading) [research-verticals].

The Media Captain's article gives landscaping a lower Local Services lead price than plumbing, heating and cooling or roofing
[mediacaptain-lsa]; the Numbers give the figures and their scope. The Numbers also set lawn care and a tree removal beside a
design-build project, whose value the research document estimates without a published source [research-verticals] [ha-lawn] [ha-tree].

Home-improvement registration. Maryland's law lists landscaping as a home improvement [md-br-8-101], New Jersey's rule lists it in
its definition of home improvement [nj-hic-def], and Pennsylvania and Connecticut name it in their definitions, Pennsylvania
excepting a landscaper its Department of Agriculture certifies unless the work includes a patio, walkway, fence, lighting, drainage,
retaining wall or another listed structure [pa-hicpa] [ct-20-427]; each asks for the number in ads, Connecticut also for the fact of
registration.

## Numbers

- Local Services landscaping leads: $39 each (The Media Captain's average, compiled from data on more than 100 of its clients in an article dated August 2025 that gives no period; the same article has $69 for plumbing, $80 for HVAC and $162 for roofing) [mediacaptain-lsa].
- Lawn care: homeowners spend around $123 on average, typically $49–$203; the page prices a mowing visit at $50–$205 (and in its FAQ at $30–$80) and elsewhere says most homeowners spend about $300, from $100 to $500 (HomeAdvisor's cost guide, page updated June 2026; the page names no region) [ha-lawn].
- A tree removal: $750 on average, in a normal range of $200–$2,000 (HomeAdvisor's cost guide, page updated June 2026; the page names no region) [ha-tree].
- A landscaping project: $3,000–$30,000 (estimate: the research document's value of one new customer, with no published source behind it; not a quote) [research-verticals].

## Do not say

- "Organic" unless the owner says so; never "pet-safe", "safe" or "nontoxic" for treatments [tx-pest-ads].
- Weed or pest treatments in Florida without a pest control license [fl-482-021] [fl-482-071].
