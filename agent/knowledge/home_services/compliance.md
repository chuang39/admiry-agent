# Home services — what the ads must not say

License numbers, verification and claims a home-services business's ads must get right, state by state.

## Key points

- Name the business and put its license or registration number in a description where the state asks (California, Florida, Arizona, Oregon, Washington, Nevada, Texas, Illinois, Maryland, New Jersey, Pennsylvania, Massachusetts, Connecticut and others) [ca-bp-7030-5] [fl-489-119] [research-verticals]; Connecticut ads also say "registered" [ct-20-427].
- Locksmiths and garage door companies need Google's Advanced Verification; their text ads carry no service-call fees, "starting at" prices, emergency or repair coupons, or fast-arrival claims not always kept [g-av].
- California ads never say "bonded" [cslb-ads] and name the coverage (such as general liability) whenever they claim insurance [ca-bp-7027-4].
- A payment amount, "<n> months", a down payment or a rate brings credit-advertising disclosures: say "financing available" [regz-ads].

## Details

Not legal advice: the state board has the final word. A number only in an ad asset may not satisfy these rules (untested; our
reading) [research-verticals].

California. The license number goes in "all forms of advertising" [ca-bp-7030-5]; the licensing board's guide counts any website
or post that solicits business and bars saying a contractor is bonded [cslb-ads]; the statute disciplines advertising "bonded"
for the license or disciplinary bond, or "insured" without the type of coverage [ca-bp-7027-4].

Florida. The number goes in each offer of services, proposal, bid, contract or advertisement, "regardless of medium" [fl-489-119];
electrical contractors too [fl-489-521].

Arizona. The number carries the prefix "ROC"; an internet ad may instead link directly to a website that prominently displays the
licensee's name and number [az-32-1124].

Oregon. All internet advertising, websites included, shows the contractor's license number [or-812-003-0120].

Washington. Advertising that shows the contractor's name or address shows the registration number; "bonded and insured" may not
be advertised because of the bond and insurance the law requires [wa-18-27-100].

Nevada. All advertising, the internet included, carries the company's name and license number [nv-624].

Texas. Air-conditioning and refrigeration [tx-ac-ads], electrical (name too) [tx-elec-ads] and plumbing [tx-plumb-ads] companies
put the license number in advertising designed to solicit their business; for plumbing, its responsible master plumber's, in any
medium.

Illinois. Every advertisement for plumbing services includes the plumbing license number [il-plumb-ads].

Home-improvement numbers. Maryland's license [md-mhic] and the registrations of New Jersey [nj-hic], Pennsylvania [pa-hicpa],
Massachusetts [ma-142a] and Connecticut [ct-20-427] go in advertisements; a Connecticut ad also says the contractor is registered.
A New Jersey electrical contractor's ads carry its name, "Electrical Contractor Bus. Permit #" with the number, and its street
address [nj-elec-ads].

Google. Garage door and locksmith services in the United States need Advanced Verification: their text ads carry no service call
fees, minimum or starting prices, or discounts and coupons for emergency or repair services; rapid-response claims unlikely to
always be true are barred, landing pages included. No other trade is listed [g-av].

Financing. Regulation Z's rule: an ad stating a payment amount, the number of payments, the repayment period, the down payment or
the finance charge must also state the down payment, the repayment terms and the annual percentage rate, as applicable; any rate,
"0%" included, is an annual percentage rate [regz-ads].

Reviews. The FTC's rule bars fake reviews, paying for reviews of a chosen sentiment, and suppressing reviews by threats
[ftc-reviews].

## Numbers

- California: a person who is not licensed may advertise construction work only if the project's total contract price for labor, material and all other items is under $1,000, and the ad must say the person is not licensed [ca-bp-7027-2].
- Google may ask a verified locksmith or garage door company to comply with re-verification once every 12 months [g-av].
- North Carolina's contractor-number rule, 21 NCAC 12A .0501(a), asks for the number on contracts and bids [nc-12a-0501]; the research document says ads and websites were dropped from it [research-verticals].

## Do not say

- "Bonded" (California), "bonded and insured" (Washington), "license" or "licensed" for New Jersey registrations [nj-hic-notice].
- "Starting at", a service-call fee or an emergency or repair coupon (locksmith, garage door).
- "There in <n> minutes" unless every call can be met.
- "Safe", "harmless" or "nontoxic" pest treatments (California and Texas bar them) [ca-ccr-1999-5] [tx-pest-ads].
