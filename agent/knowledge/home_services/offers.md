# Home services — what the ads lead with

Offers that move homeowners to call or ask for a quote, and the rules each brings.

## Key points

- Lead with the offer the owner told Admiry; never invent a price or a discount.
- Projects, if the owner offers them: a free estimate or consultation, financing, a warranty [research-verticals].
- Repairs: a fast response only when true, upfront pricing, the license or registration number where required.
- Storm and water-damage repair ads never invite an insurance claim or offer to file, handle, help with or negotiate one, cover or waive a deductible [fl-489-147] [tx-ins-4102-163] [tx-bc-27-02] [tdi-roofing].
- Maintenance plans can make one visit a yearly customer: heating and cooling, plumbing, pest control [research-verticals].
- Locksmiths and garage door companies: no coupons or discounts in text ads for emergency or repair work [g-av].

## Details

What a customer is worth decides how much an offer can cost; each trade's file gives its estimates. Projects (remodels, roofs,
windows, siding, landscaping) lead with whichever of a free estimate or consultation, financing and a warranty the owner offers:
the research document gives them a free consultation or a quote request as call to action, and lists estimate booking, financing
and warranties among their page modules. Repairs lead with a fast response only when it is true, upfront pricing
and the license or registration number where the state asks for it, as the compliance file says [research-verticals].

Maintenance plans. A heating and cooling plan can make one repair a yearly customer; the research document lists plans on plumbing,
pest control and landscaping pages [research-verticals].

Financing. Regulation Z's advertising rule: an ad that states a payment amount, the number of payments, the repayment period, the
down payment or the finance charge must also state the down payment, the terms of repayment and the annual percentage rate, as
applicable, and any rate, "0%" included, is stated as an annual percentage rate [regz-ads]. Our reading is that a monthly amount
stated for financing advertises credit, so the ad says "financing available" and leaves the amount out [research-verticals].

Utility rebates only while the utility offers them; same-day slots only when the schedule holds one [research-verticals].

Insurance claims. After a storm or a loss, a roofing or water-damage repair ad names the repair and a written estimate. It does
not invite a claim, offer to file, handle, help with or negotiate one, to cover or waive the deductible, or to promise that
insurance pays: Florida bars a contractor that is not a licensed public adjuster from adjusting an insured's claim, limits ads
inviting a roof-damage claim, and bars offering a deductible waiver for a roof inspection or claim, and Texas bars advertising to
adjust claims or to pay, waive or absorb a deductible without the insurer's consent [fl-489-147] [tx-ins-4102-163] [tx-bc-27-02];
Texas's insurance department calls offering to file a claim for the policyholder improper [tdi-roofing]. The roofing file has the
rules; our reading is that they reach water-damage repair too.

Locksmiths and garage door companies. Google bars discounts and coupons in text ads that promote emergency or repair services;
a warranty, coupon, discount or flat-rate offer named in an ad or page needs its terms, or what it includes and excludes, on that
page [g-av].

## Numbers

- Heating and cooling maintenance plans: $150–$300 a year (estimate) [research-verticals].

## Do not say

- "$<amount> a month" or "<n>% financing" without the credit terms.
- "Your insurance will pay for it."
- "We cover your deductible."
- A discount the owner did not give.
