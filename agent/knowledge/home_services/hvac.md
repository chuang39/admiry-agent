# Home services — heating and cooling

How heating and cooling ads catch breakdowns, replacements and maintenance plans, and which searches to keep out.

## Key points

- Three jobs: a breakdown (no cooling or no heat: a call now), a replacement (a quote and financing), a maintenance plan (our reading) [research-verticals].
- Lead with cooling in the hot months and heating in the cold ones.
- Keep out: HVAC jobs, technician schools and certification, parts, thermostat manuals, window units for sale.
- A Texas air-conditioning contracting company's ads carry its affiliated licensee's license number [tx-ac-ads]; Pennsylvania asks a heating and cooling contractor for its home-improvement registration number in ads [pa-hicpa].

## Details

A breakdown (no cooling or no heat) is a call now: the ad shows the call button and, when the hours cover it, the word
"emergency". A replacement is a quote with financing, and a maintenance plan turns one repair into a yearly customer. The research
document lists an emergency banner, a maintenance-club signup, financing and utility rebates, and a replacement estimate form among
a heating and cooling page's modules [research-verticals]. A rebate goes in an ad only while it is current; the federal credit for
heat pumps, central air and furnaces ended with 2025 [irs-25c].

Financing. Our reading is that an ad giving a payment amount, "<n> months", a down payment or a rate for financing brings
Regulation Z's credit-advertising disclosures, so say "financing available" and leave them out; the compliance file gives the
rule [regz-ads].

Search interest. The research document read "hvac near me" at 150 on Google Trends against 100 for "plumber near me"; the figure
is relative, and the term also catches job searches [research-verticals]; in our reading, that is why the negative list (jobs,
technician schools, certification) matters.

Texas. The rule asks that all advertising by an air-conditioning and refrigeration contracting company designed to solicit that
business include the affiliated licensee's license number. Its exemptions, some on conditions, are nationally placed television (a
statement that license numbers are available on request), telephone-book listings of name, address and telephone number only, the
trade ads manufacturers and distributors place in telephone books, telephone solicitations (the caller says the company complies
with state licensing and gives the number on request), items of nominal value, office letterheads and printed forms, and signs at
the permanent business location; none names the internet, so our reading is that a search ad carries the number [tx-ac-ads].

Pennsylvania. The registration reaches any contractor with $5,000 or more of home improvements in the previous taxable year;
home improvement includes repair, replacement and installation and names central heating and air conditioning; the Act's
"advertisement" names the Internet [pa-hicpa].

## Numbers

- Air Conditioning Installation & Repair's average cost per lead in search advertising: $127.74; Heating & Furnaces: $129.02 (LocaliQ's 2025 home-services report: its customers' US search campaigns on Google Ads and Microsoft Ads, 3,211 in all, at least 103 in each category, April 2024 to March 2025; its "averages" are medians) [liq-home-2026].
- Installing or replacing an HVAC system: around $7,500 on average, with most homeowners paying $5,000–$12,500 (HomeAdvisor's cost guide, page updated June 2026; the page names no region) [ha-hvac].
- An AC repair: most homeowners pay $417, typically $172–$664 (HomeAdvisor's cost guide, page updated June 2026; the page names no region) [ha-ac-repair].
- A furnace repair: an average of $318, in a normal range of $133–$505 (HomeAdvisor's cost guide, page updated June 2026; the page names no region) [ha-furnace-repair].
- Average ticket (revenue per paying customer) from HVAC Local Services leads: $2,110 (SearchLight's benchmark, February 2026: 409 HVAC accounts in a sample of 888 home-services contractors; the page calls February heating season and says it contributes to both the strong conversion rates and the higher tickets) [searchlight-lsa].
- Local Services HVAC leads: $80 each (The Media Captain's average, compiled from data on more than 100 of its clients in an article dated August 2025 that gives no period); SearchLight's February 2026 benchmark has $51 a lead for HVAC [mediacaptain-lsa] [searchlight-lsa].
- "hvac near me" ran 150 on Google Trends against 100 for "plumber near me" (US, the 12 months to 2026-10-05; relative interest, not search counts; the term also catches job searches) [research-verticals].

## Do not say

- A federal tax credit for a heat pump, furnace or central air (ended) [irs-25c].
